Science-Backed (10)

Supplement Type Why it may help energy Evidence quality Sources
IronNon-StimulantCorrects low iron status and can reduce fatigue when iron deficiency is present.High (deficiency-specific)[1], [2]
Vitamin B12Non-StimulantPrevents megaloblastic anemia and tiredness in deficiency states.High (deficiency-specific)[3]
Folate (B9)Non-StimulantSupports DNA and red blood cell production; deficiency can cause fatigue and weakness.High (deficiency-specific)[4]
Vitamin DNon-StimulantLow vitamin D is linked to fatigue in some populations; correction can help in deficiency.Moderate (deficiency-specific)[5]
MagnesiumNon-StimulantRequired for ATP-related cellular functions; low status can include fatigue/weakness symptoms.Moderate (deficiency-specific)[6]
Creatine monohydrateNon-StimulantRaises phosphocreatine stores, improving short-burst energy and repeated high-intensity performance.High (exercise context)[7]
Beta-alanineNon-StimulantIncreases muscle carnosine buffering, helping delay fatigue in hard efforts.Moderate to High (exercise context)[7]
Beetroot (dietary nitrate)Non-StimulantCan improve oxygen efficiency and endurance in some protocols.Moderate (exercise context)[7]
CaffeineStimulantAdenosine receptor antagonism improves alertness and reduces perceived effort/fatigue acutely.High (acute effect)[7], [8]
Green tea extract (EGCG + caffeine)StimulantProvides mild stimulant effect and may support alertness; effect size is usually smaller than pure caffeine.Moderate[8], [9]

Anecdotal or Mixed Evidence (10)

Supplement Type Why people use it for energy Evidence quality Sources
CoQ10Non-StimulantMitochondrial cofactor; commonly used for cellular energy, but performance findings are mixed.Mixed to Moderate[10]
AshwagandhaNon-StimulantMostly used for stress/sleep support, which may indirectly improve daytime energy.Mixed (small studies)[11], [12]
Rhodiola roseaNon-StimulantTraditional fatigue herb; human evidence exists but quality is inconsistent.Low to Moderate[13], [14]
L-carnitineNon-StimulantInvolved in fatty acid transport; tends to help most in select clinical contexts.Mixed (condition-specific)[15]
Panax ginsengNon-StimulantAdaptogen with some fatigue/endurance signals; heterogenous trials and products.Mixed[16]
CordycepsNon-StimulantPopular in endurance stacks; RCT support is limited and variable.Low[17]
MacaNon-StimulantOften reported by users as an energy/libido support, but rigorous fatigue endpoints are sparse.Low[18]
Eleuthero (Siberian ginseng)Non-StimulantHistorically used for stamina and fatigue, with limited modern high-quality confirmation.Low to Moderate[19]
L-theanineNon-StimulantUsually used with caffeine for smoother alertness; alone is more calming than energizing.Mixed[20]
B-complex blendsNon-StimulantVery common anecdotal energy strategy; strongest rationale is correcting low B-vitamin status.Mixed[3], [4], [21]
Research note: the strongest gains are usually seen when a real deficiency exists (iron, B12, folate, vitamin D) or in specific performance contexts (creatine, beta-alanine, dietary nitrate, caffeine).
