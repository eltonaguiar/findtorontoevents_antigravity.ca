1. Credential at a glance

CIS-CSM is ServiceNow’s implementation-specialist credential for the Customer Service Management application. The official blueprint states the exam confirms you can configure, implement, and maintain the ServiceNow CSM application, and validates your ability to manage data models, configure case routing, oversee case processes, and use CSM workspaces, portals, and analytics.

Recommended experience is hands-on: practical experience implementing or maintaining CSM in ServiceNow instances, plus working knowledge of customer service processes, industry data structures, and case workflows. Treat the blueprint as the authority for scope and the credential page as the authority for eligibility and registration.

Customer data → entitlement → intake channel → routed case → task and escalation work → resolution, review, and reporting

Domain 1: CSM foundational data model — 27%

Business models

CSM supports more than one shape of customer. In a B2B model the Account is the anchor: contacts, contracts, entitlements, sold products, teams, and escalations all hang off it. In a B2C model the Consumer and Household records carry that weight, and consumers self-register through the Consumer Service Portal. A B2B2C model layers consumers inside a customer organization, which is why the customer data models for B2B2C plugin depends on install base management. Account relationships are stored as records with a source account and a target account plus a relationship type, so a partner, reseller, or parent company link is data you can query, not a note on a form. Decide the model before you import anything: choosing households versus accounts late means rebuilding contacts, access rules, and portal visibility.

Product models

The product side of the model describes what a customer bought and what the service commitment is: product and service definitions, product models, sold products, and the service offerings they connect to. Product context is what makes a case specific — a case about a particular model, version, or offering can inherit the right templates, knowledge, and entitlement checks instead of relying on the agent to ask. Service Model Foundation is the current framing for entities such as business locations and households, and ServiceNow has renamed several table and field labels across recent releases, so study the current labels rather than an older course screenshot. Keep the alignment with the CMDB and CSDM in mind: product and asset identity should reconcile, not fork into a second inventory.

Install base management

Install base management captures the current state of what a customer has in service and the relationships that affect how it works. It is delivered by its own plugin, and install base items sit alongside assets and configuration items rather than replacing them: an install base item records the customer’s instance of a product, product instances can reference an asset or a CI, and sold products populate the base. That relationship is the difference between “this account bought plan X” and “these four units are deployed at site Y and unit 3 is the one generating the case.” Expect exam items that test whether you can trace from a case to the affected installed item and its related entities.

Contracts and entitlements

Contracts record the commercial relationship, contract contacts record who is covered, entitlements record what coverage applies, and case entitlements link that coverage to an individual case through the Entitlements related list. Entitlement is the field a careful implementation checks before promising response times or offering a channel that costs money, and the advanced entitlements system property controls how that related list is displayed. Expect questions that separate the contract from the entitlement and the entitlement from the case: one contract can cover many contacts, one entitlement can apply to many cases, and a case without a matched entitlement is a data-quality signal, not a silent pass.

Practice: Draw a B2B2C chain on paper: account, relationship to a partner account, contact, contract, contract contact, entitlement, sold product, and install base item. Mark which record you would inspect to answer “is this contact covered, and which physical unit is affected?”

Check your understanding: is a contract the same thing as an entitlement?

No. The contract is the commercial agreement, the entitlement expresses the coverage that agreement grants, and the case entitlement record ties that coverage to a specific case. Contracts, entitlements, and cases are separate records with their own relationships.

Domain 2: CSM configuration — 38%

CSM in ServiceNow

CSM is delivered as plugins on the platform: a Customer Service base plugin with base entities (accounts, contacts, consumers, entitlements), plus a long list of optional feature plugins for case types, case digests, major issue management, install base, portals, walk-up, playbooks, mobility, virtual agent topics, and integrations with ITSM, request, field service, and SPM. Some plugins are demo data and belong only in non-production instances. The platform services CSM leans on — users, groups, roles, access controls, service catalog, SLAs, notifications, flow designer, and reporting — are the same ones you learned as an administrator, so most configuration mistakes are platform mistakes wearing a CSM hat. Guided setup is the documented path through activation and configuration; there is a checklist for it later on this page.

Routing and assignment

Routing turns an inbound request into owned work. Service channels, presence states, work item queues, Advanced Work Assignment eligibility and reject reasons, and assignment rules decide who sees what and when. Email channel configuration records control how inbound mail becomes an interaction or a case; assignment and routing rules decide the group and agent. The design question is always operational: route to the team that can actually resolve the issue for that account, in that region, in that entitlement tier. Test with the intended agent and supervisor roles, not only with an administrator account, and check for queues that never drain, agents who never receive work because of presence or eligibility settings, and rules that fire on the wrong case type.

Case types

A case type is a configured business process, implemented as a table that extends the Case table, plus the definition record that registers it. This is the deepest configuration surface in the exam, and it is heavily documented: create the extended table in a scope other than global, set view rules, create choice records and state flows for the states, add UI policies, client scripts, business rules, and declarative actions, then register the case type and configure its related lists (state flows, special handling notes, SLAs, email templates, quick messages, reports). Then secure it: roles for agents, ACLs for external customers, and Get Case Types flow conditions so agents only see the case types they may create. Configure one Create Case declarative action, not both, and use record producers to expose the type on the portal.

Channels

CSM meets customers where they are: email, chat, telephony and messaging, web and portal forms, walk-up locations, social channels, and Engagement Messenger embedded on an external site, with contact center integrations for enterprise telephony. Each channel has its own configuration, its own routing implications, and its own cost of failure — an email channel that silently creates duplicates, an unauthenticated chat that never reaches a queue, a portal record producer that bypasses entitlement checks. Know which channels Advanced Work Assignment can serve for CSM, and know that the same issue arriving through two channels should converge on one case where the product supports it, rather than fragmenting the customer’s history.

Practice: Design one case type end to end for a refund request: name the extended table, list the states in its state flow, the UI policy you would add, the role that can create it, the ACL that lets an authenticated portal user view it, and the channel you would expose it on.

Check your understanding: does a case type require a new table?

Yes. Each case type creates a table extending sn_customerservice_case, which inherits most Case behavior, and a case type definition record in sn_case_type registers it. View rules, state flows, UI policies, client scripts, business rules, and declarative actions are then configured against that table.

Official reading: Configuring customer service case types; Additional plugins for Customer Service Management; Configuring Customer Service Management overview; CSM Best Practices for Implementation.

Domain 3: Case management — 17%

Case state flows

The Case table extends Task, so cases share the task family’s state machinery. State flows define which transitions are allowed between states, and you can configure work to happen during a transition. The blueprint’s own sample question is a useful anchor: the final state of a case is Closed, not Resolved or Completed. For a case type you create choice records for each state on that case type’s table with Element set to State, then build the flow. The exam cares that you understand state is configured behavior with business meaning, not a free-text field an agent edits.

Major issue management

Major issue management is a capability, delivered by its own plugin, for customer-facing communications and resolution work on issues that affect many customers at once. One root cause generates a major issue, related cases are associated with it, and communication is managed centrally instead of each agent improvising. The Case form carries a major case section whose visibility is controlled by an inherited UI policy. Know when a spike of similar cases should become a major issue, who owns the customer communication, and how resolution of the major issue relates to the individual cases.

Case digests and service definitions

Case digests let an agent send periodic summaries of progress while a case is open, and produce a post case review with root cause, mitigation, and preventive actions when work completes. Case action summaries and post case reviews are configured per case type through the Case Digests configuration and mapped in the CSM table map table. Service definitions connect what was sold and committed to the case process so the right templates, SLAs, and knowledge are available. Both features need their plugins, and both are configured per case type rather than globally — a common implementation mistake is configuring the base case only and assuming new types inherit it.

CSM workspace

The agent experience lives in a workspace: the configurable CSM workspace plugin delivers the UI Builder version of the service desk, and CRM Workspace configuration covers lists, notifications, highlighted values, ribbon settings, form headers, and action assignments — including per case type. The point of a workspace is consolidation: case, account, entitlement, related records, knowledge, and channel history in one place, with customer central giving a single view of a customer’s information and activity. Expect items about which pane shows what, and about configuring a workspace differently for different case types.

Case actions, tasks, and escalations

Case actions are the buttons, links, and menu items on forms and lists; declarative actions are the supported way to add them, and inherited actions can be blocked. Case tasks are the child work records for the actual resolution steps, so a case can be split across teams without losing the parent thread. Escalations have their own records with severity definitions and templates that drive notification and priority when a case breaches expectations. Related party and responsibility records add the B2B nuance: which entity — account, contact, or consumer — is responsible for what on this case. Assignments and escalations should be tested with representative cases, not assumed from configuration.

Practice: Take a case that has breached its response commitment. Write down the records involved in order: case, entitlement check, task, escalation record, escalation severity, related party responsibility, and the workspace view an agent would use to recover it.

Check your understanding: is Resolved the end of a case?

No. Resolved is a working state; the final state of a case is Closed. State flows define the permitted transitions, and closure rules determine how a case gets there.

Official reading: Set up CRM Workspace; Tables installed with Customer Service Management; Case API reference; Knowledge Management for Customer Service Management Fundamentals.

Domain 4: Workspace, Portals, Service Catalog, Analytics — 8%

Portals and service catalog

Self-service runs through portals: the Customer Service Portal for authenticated customers, the Consumer Service Portal for B2C consumers, and configurable portal widgets for layout and behavior. Record producers expose case types on a portal so a customer can raise the right kind of case without an agent mediating. Knowledge, communities, service catalog, chatbots, appointment booking, and walk-up check-in all live on this side of the boundary. The catalog connection matters twice: as the intake path for requests, and as the service definition context that tells the case what was sold.

Special handling notes, SLAs, and targeted communications

Special handling notes annotate a case with standing instructions — a VIP flag, a regulatory caveat, a site access restriction — and are configured per case type through the related list. SLAs on cases and case types set the commitments the case must meet, which is what makes escalation and reporting meaningful rather than decorative. Targeted communications is the supported way to publish and distribute content and articles to internal and external audiences. Together these three are the “context around the case” layer: instructions, commitments, and outbound messaging.

Platform Analytics and reporting

Reporting answers whether the implementation works: case volume and aging by channel, first-contact resolution, SLA attainment, deflection on the portal, and agent workload. Case report records store key performance indicators and metrics for cases, and Performance Analytics content packs for Customer Service (plus an advanced pack tied to major issue management, case action status, AWA, and related plugins) supply the out-of-the-box indicators. Performance Analytics is licensed separately, and some content packs display incorrectly if their dependency plugins are missing. Build a small set of reports you trust, then drill into the underlying records to confirm filters and scopes before anyone presents the numbers.

Practice: Pick one executive question — “how many cases did we deflect last month?” — and name the record or indicator you would use, the plugins it depends on, and the filter you would verify first.

Check your understanding: do all CSM indicators work as soon as CSM is installed?

No. Performance Analytics content packs depend on their own activation and on related feature plugins, and PA itself is a separate license for collecting indicator scores. Missing dependencies show up as blank or incorrect indicators rather than as errors.

Official reading: Set up self-service and extend capabilities; CSM plugins including PA content packs.

Domain 5: CSM Best Practices and Knowledge Management — 10%

Best practice: ServiceNow architecture

The architecture question on the exam is really an upgrade question: does your solution survive the next release? Prefer configuration over customization, keep extensions in an application scope rather than global, extend the case table instead of forking the data model, and reuse platform capabilities — flow designer, declarative actions, UI policies, scoped apps — before writing scripts. Align customer, product, and service data with CSDM and the CMDB so the CSM model and the platform model do not diverge. The blueprint’s own sample question asks which methodology ServiceNow uses to manage CSM implementation projects; the answer is Now Create, so know what that methodology asks you to do.

Best practice: customer service end-to-end processes

Design the whole loop, not the case form: intake through the right channel, identification and entitlement verification, triage and routing, resolution with tasks and escalations, communication while work is in progress, closure with a post case review, and measurement afterwards. Each handoff is where implementations fail — an accurate case with an unknown owner is still stuck. Walk a real scenario with the customer’s process owners and mark where responsibility transfers, because that is where your assignment rules, responsibilities, and notifications must line up.

Best practice: defining project scope

Scope decides what gets configured first. The implementation workshop material frames it as reviewing a statement of work, confirming the purchased package, and working from a capability matrix — business models to customer data models, industry data models, security, governance, and organizational change management. A defensible scope names the case types, channels, portals, integrations, and reporting that are in, the release and plugins being used, and who accepts each piece. Scope creep in CSM usually arrives as “one more case type,” which is cheap to say and expensive to secure, test, and document.

Knowledge management

Knowledge in CSM is customer-facing infrastructure: knowledge bases and articles that serve both agents and portal users, with access controlled by roles, ACLs, and product entitlements that restrict which articles a given customer can see. The Article Quality Index is the published measure of article quality, so treat it as a maintenance target rather than a vanity metric. Knowledge blocks let you reuse a controlled snippet of content across articles, and search configuration determines whether an agent or a customer finds the right article at the moment of need — contextual search on the case form uses the case knowledge base search context. Bulk translations support multilingual portals without maintaining parallel manual copies.

Practice: Take a case type you know and write the end-to-end process in ten steps, then mark each step as “platform configuration,” “data,” or “organizational change.” Anything you marked that nobody owns is a scope gap.

Check your understanding: which implementation methodology does ServiceNow use for CSM projects?

Now Create. The blueprint’s sample item lists Agile, SIM, Now Create, and OCM, with Now Create as the answer — and the credential’s recommended preparation includes the “Get Started with Now Create” course.

Official reading: Get Started with the Now Create Methodology; CSM Implementation Workshop syllabus; Contextual search for case types.

Getting started: the Guided Setup wizard and plugins

Yes — CSM has an in-product setup wizard. The documentation calls it Customer Service guided setup, reached from Customer Service > Administration > Guided Setup, and it walks administrators through the entire setup and configuration process in categories (getting started, case types, workspace, integration, and so on). The role called out in the case-type documentation is csm_guided_setup_user or admin. Guided setup reduces the chance of missing a step; it does not replace entitlement checks, prerequisites, or release-matched documentation.

  1. Confirm entitlement and release: licensed CSM application, a compatible ServiceNow release, administrator rights to activate plugins, and the target release’s documentation (release families are renamed regularly — check which one your instance runs).
  2. Activate the base plugins: Customer Service plus customer service base entities, then the feature plugins you actually need: case types, case digests, major issue management, install base, portals, walk-up, playbooks, and the Service Model Foundation plugins for business locations and households.
  3. Add demo data only where it belongs: the CSM demo data plugin and other demo plugins are for non-production instances, so you can exercise features without touching real customers.
  4. Run guided setup: import foundation data (accounts, contacts, users, groups), configure access controls, then work through the guided setup categories for case types, workspace, channels, and portals.
  5. Prove it end to end: use the documented quick start tests for CSM, then create a case through each channel, verify routing, entitlement, tasks, escalation, portal visibility, and reporting with the intended agent and customer roles.

Start with the official Configuring Customer Service Management page, then the case type configuration procedure (which documents the guided setup entry point) and the quick start tests for CSM. Use the documentation selector so the pages match the release installed on your instance.

Plugin identifiers matter when you talk to an administrator: the base plugin is com.sn_customerservice, case types are com.snc.csm_case_types, case digests com.sn_csm_case_digest, major issue management com.sn_majorissue_mgt, install base com.snc.install_base, the configurable workspace sn_csm_wrkspc, and walk-up for CSM com.snc.walkup_for_csm.

Implementation sequence

Use this checklist to structure a first implementation or a focused design review:

  1. Set the foundation: confirm release, entitlement, plugins, roles, and the customer and product data the process depends on.
  2. Model the customer: choose B2B, B2C, or B2B2C deliberately; import accounts, contacts, consumers, relationships, and access rules before configuring anything customer-visible.
  3. Attach coverage: load contracts, entitlements, sold products, and install base items, then verify a case can be matched to them.
  4. Configure work: create case types, state flows, routing and assignment, channels, workspace views, tasks, and escalations in a non-production instance.
  5. Expose self-service: portals, record producers, knowledge bases, and entitlement-aware visibility for external users.
  6. Exercise end to end: one case through every channel, plus an escalated and a major-issue case, inspected with agent, supervisor, and customer roles.
  7. Operate and improve: review SLA attainment, aging queues, knowledge quality index, deflection, and entitlement mismatches; revise configuration from observed cases.

This is a study framework, not a ServiceNow-prescribed deployment procedure. Follow official product documentation and your organization’s policies.
