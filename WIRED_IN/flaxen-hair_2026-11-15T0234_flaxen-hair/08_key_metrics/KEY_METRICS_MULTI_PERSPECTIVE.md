# Key Metrics from Multiple Perspectives

## Shared Baseline
Any strategy considered for capital must show positive expectancy after costs and a reasonable sample size.

## Sharpe Ratio Reference Points
- S&P 500 long-run: roughly 0.4–0.6
- Good equity mutual fund: ~1.0
- Strong hedge fund: ~1.5–2.0+
- Claims above 3–4 require extreme scrutiny (often high-frequency backtests, short samples, or errors)

## Perspective-Specific Thresholds

### Pro Investor / Allocator
- Sharpe ≥ 0.8–1.0 net over multi-year period
- Max drawdown manageable relative to return
- Low correlation to existing portfolio
- Clear capacity estimate
- Preference for strategies with economic rationale

### Mutual Fund Manager
- Must justify active risk vs benchmark (information ratio)
- Expense ratio competitive
- Tracking error controlled if claiming to be enhanced index
- Consistency across market regimes preferred over one spectacular year
- Sharpe often secondary to absolute and relative performance vs peers and benchmark

### Bank / Prop Desk Top Trader
- High Sharpe possible on short-horizon books, but risk limits are tight
- Focus on daily/weekly P&L volatility and VaR
- Strong preference for strategies that can be risk-managed in real time
- Cost and execution quality are first-order concerns

### Risk Manager
- Max drawdown, Calmar, Sortino, probability of ruin
- Stress tests across regimes
- Position sizing rules that survive worst historical sequences
- Independent kill switches

## Minimum Gates for This System (Recommended)
- Cost-adjusted profit factor ≥ 1.15
- Sharpe (annualized, net) ≥ 0.5 (target ≥ 1.0)
- Forward or OOS n ≥ 100 (target ≥ 500 for promotion)
- Beats relevant buy-and-hold benchmark
- Max drawdown within predefined limit
- Positive expectancy and IC distinguishable from zero

These are stricter than many retail backtests but closer to what would be required before anyone trusts real capital.
