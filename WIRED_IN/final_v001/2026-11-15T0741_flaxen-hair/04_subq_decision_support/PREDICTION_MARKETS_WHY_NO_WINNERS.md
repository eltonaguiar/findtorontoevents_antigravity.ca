# Why Kalshi / Polymarket Copy Trading Has Not Produced Durable Winners

## Structural Reasons (Not Just Bad Luck)

1. **Zero-sum (or worse after fees)**  
   Every dollar of profit is someone else's loss, minus platform fees. Unlike equities, there is no underlying economic growth or risk premium being shared.

2. **Professional / bot dominance**  
   Multiple analyses (WSJ, Dune, academic) show a tiny fraction of accounts (often <1%, frequently bots) capture the majority of profits. Retail accounts are systematically on the losing side.

3. **Adverse selection and latency**  
   Informed or faster traders pick off slower quotes. Stanford Law analysis of Kalshi trades documented measurable adverse selection. Retail copy-traders arrive late and at worse prices.

4. **Favorite-longshot bias and behavioral errors**  
   Retail overpays for low-probability high-payout contracts. Pros sell those overpriced contracts.

5. **Copy-trading implementation problems**  
   - Size mismatch (whale risk vs your bankroll).  
   - Delayed fills.  
   - Survivorship bias in "top wallets" lists.  
   - Many apparent winners have short or lucky track records.

6. **Fees and microstructure**  
   Maker-taker dynamics favor sophisticated participants. Retail is usually the taker.

## Evidence Summary
- Ordinary Kalshi users lost hundreds of millions in aggregate in analyses covering the platform's history.
- On Polymarket, 0.1% of accounts captured ~67% of profits in one major study; majority of wallets lost money.
- High forecasting accuracy does not guarantee profit if execution is poor (you can be right on direction and still lose to adverse selection and costs).

## Implication for This System
Do not expect simple copy-trading of prediction-market wallets to become a reliable profit center. Any edge would require being the informed/maker side or exploiting structural inefficiencies with speed and capital that retail-scale agents typically lack. Treat it as a research side-quest, not a core path.
