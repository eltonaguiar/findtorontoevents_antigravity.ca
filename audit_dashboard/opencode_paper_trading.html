What does "CANDIDATE" mean?

A CANDIDATE is a strategy that shows a positive edge in historical data (win rate > 50%, profit factor > 1.0, bootstrap confidence > 95%) but has NOT yet passed all institutional tests (independence, cost-adjusted expectancy, monthly consistency, max drawdown < 20%, Sharpe > 1.0). It is being paper-traded to see if the edge holds going forward. It is NOT a proven winner and should not be sized with real capital.

PROVEN = passes all 6 layers of the god-like framework (data integrity, statistical significance, OOS stability, independence/execution, regime/concentration, forward validation). Currently no strategy is PROVEN.

Statistical measures used (with ELI5)

Profit Factor (PF)
PF = total wins / total losses. PF > 1.0 means you make more than you lose. Institutional target > 1.25. Our candidate: 1.57 (aggregate), 1.20 (one-position).
Bootstrap (10,000 resamples)
We shuffle the trades 10,000 times. If the shuffled version almost never beats the real strategy, the edge is real. P(>0) = 1.0 means 100% confidence the edge is positive. Our candidate: P=1.0.
One-position-at-a-time
Simulate holding only one trade at a time. If the edge disappears, it was just overlapping bets, not real skill. Our candidate: PF drops from 1.57 to 1.20 after costs.
Max drawdown
The biggest drop from peak equity. If it's 85%, one bad streak wipes you out. Target < 20%. Our candidate: 85.58% — FAIL.
Sharpe ratio
Return per unit of risk (volatility). >1.0 good, >2.0 excellent. Our candidate: 0.19 — FAIL.
Calmar ratio
Annual return / max drawdown. Tells if gains are worth the pain. Target > 0.5. Our candidate: 1.71 — PASS.
CVaR (worst 5%)
Average loss on the worst 5% of days. Tells you how bad the bleeding gets. Our candidate: -5.31% — MARGINAL.
Monte Carlo p-value
Shuffle trade returns (flip signs) 10,000 times. If almost none beat the real strategy, p < 0.01. Our candidate: p=0.0 — PASS.
Deflated Sharpe (DSR)
Adjusts Sharpe downward for testing many strategies (data mining). DSR > 0 means the edge survives selection bias. Our candidate: -2.99 — FAIL.
Distinct exit dates
If 100 wins all exited on the same day at the same price, the data is corrupt (frozen-exit bug). We check wins ≤ 2× distinct exits. Our candidate: 54 wins / 53 exits — PASS (non-artifact).

ELI5: How we test if a pick is real (not a coin flip)

Click to expand beginner-friendly explanation

1. Win rate alone is not enough. A coin flip has 50% win rate. We need wins bigger than losses.

2. Profit factor (PF) = total wins / total losses. PF > 1.0 means you make more than you lose.

3. Bootstrap test: Shuffle trades 10,000 times. If shuffled almost never beats real, edge is real.

4. One-position-at-a-time: Hold only one trade. If edge disappears, it was overlapping bets.

5. Cost check: Subtract fees. If edge disappears after fees, not tradable.

6. Max drawdown: Biggest drop from peak. 85% = one bad streak wipes you out. Target < 20%.

7. Sharpe ratio: Return per unit of risk. >1.0 good, >2.0 excellent. Our candidate: 0.19 — FAIL.

8. Distinct exit dates: 100 wins on same day at same price = corrupt data. We check this.

Conclusion: No strategy passes all tests. Best candidate (luxalgo_confluence SHORT) has real but small, fragile edge. Needs 100+ forward trades to confirm.

ELI5: What is a "candidate" vs "proven"?

Click to expand

Candidate: A strategy that has shown a positive edge in historical data (win rate > 50%, profit factor > 1.0, bootstrap confidence > 95%) but has NOT yet passed all institutional tests. Think of it like a student who scored well on practice exams but hasn't taken the final. It is being paper-traded (fake money) to see if the edge holds in real time.

Proven: A strategy that passes ALL 6 layers of the god-like framework (data integrity, statistical significance, out-of-sample stability, independence/execution, regime/concentration, forward validation). Currently NO strategy is proven. Think of it as a student who aced the final, the oral exam, and the real-world internship.

Analogy: A candidate is a recipe that tastes great in your kitchen. A proven strategy is a recipe that tastes great in 100 different kitchens with 100 different cooks. We are still in the "one kitchen" phase.
