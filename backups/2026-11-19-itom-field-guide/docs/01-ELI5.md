## The building analogy

Imagine a large office building.

- The **building manager** needs to know every room, every wire, every lift, and who is
  allowed in each area.
- The **fire panel** needs to know when a smoke detector goes off, which floor it is on, and
  whether forty detectors are reporting the same fire.
- The **maintenance desk** needs a work order, not a raw detector reading.
- The **procurement team** needs to know what equipment exists, who owns it, and whether it
  follows the building code.

ITOM is the building manager, the fire panel, and the maintenance desk for IT.

| Building idea | ITOM idea |
|---|---|
| The building | Your IT infrastructure and services |
| The asset register | The **CMDB** (Configuration Management Database) |
| The census taker who walks the building | **Discovery** |
| The wiring diagram that shows which rooms feed which service | **Service Mapping** |
| A smoke detector reading | An **event** |
| The fire panel showing one light for forty detectors | An **alert** |
| A work order to fix it | An **incident** |
| A camera recording everything that happened | A **log**; **Health Log Analytics** reads it |
| A heart-rate monitor | A **metric**; **Metric Intelligence** watches it |
| A promise that the lift is available 99.9% of the month | An **SLO** (service level objective) |
| The fire drill that proves the alarm works | **Synthetic monitoring** |
| A purchase request and a building permit | **Cloud Accelerate** |

## The five words you must not mix up

### CI — configuration item

Anything you manage: a server, a router, a database, an application, a service, a
certificate. In ServiceNow it is a record in a CMDB table such as `cmdb_ci_server` or
`cmdb_ci_appl`.

**Why it matters:** almost every ITOM answer is "which CI?" If the CI is wrong, the alert,
the impact, and the report are wrong.

### CMDB — Configuration Management Database

The database of CIs **and their relationships**. Think of it as an asset register plus a
family tree. It is not a flat inventory: relationships are what let an alert on a router
become an impact on a customer-facing service.

### Event — the raw reading

A record that something happened, produced by a monitoring tool, a device, a log, or a
metric. Events are stored in the Event [`em_event`] table. Most events are boring; that is
normal.

### Alert — the work item

The deduplicated, enriched, prioritised version of one or more events. Alerts are stored in
the Alert [`em_alert`] table, are bound to a CI, and are what a human acknowledges, groups,
and resolves.

**The one-sentence difference:** an event is what the machine reported; an alert is what the
NOC works.

### Incident — the ticket

The ITIL record a team works to restore service. Event Management can open an incident
automatically when an alert matches an alert management rule.

```
source → event (em_event) → event rule → alert (em_alert) → alert management rule → incident
```

## What each ITOM product is for

| Product | One sentence | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Scans the network, finds devices and applications, and updates the CMDB | What exists? |
| **Service Mapping** | Follows connections from an entry point to build a map of the service | What depends on what? |
| **Event Management** | Collects events, creates alerts, groups them, and triggers a response | What is wrong, and who should work it? |
| **Health Log Analytics** | Reads logs, learns normal patterns, and raises an alert when behaviour is anomalous | What do the logs say, and what changed? |
| **Metric Intelligence** | Watches metric history and flags anomalies that events may miss | Is this number abnormal for this CI? |
| **Service Reliability Management** | Gives teams a workflow to respond, collaborate, track, and remediate | Who owns the reliability work? |
| **Service Observability** | Brings third-party telemetry (Datadog, Dynatrace, etc.) next to CMDB service data | What is the full-stack health of this service? |
| **Synthetic Monitoring** | Calls an HTTP endpoint on a schedule and alerts when it fails or is slow | Is the service reachable before a user complains? |
| **SLO Management** | Defines SLIs, SLOs, and error budgets and tracks them | Are we meeting the promise? |
| **Cloud Accelerate** | Requests, accounts for, and governs cloud resources | What should exist in cloud, who owns it, and is it compliant? |
| **Agent Client Collector** | Runs an agent on a host to collect inventory, metrics, and logs | What is happening inside a machine we cannot scan agentlessly? |
| **AI in ITOM** | Assists with mapping, summarisation, generation, and operations | Can AI reduce the manual work without hiding the evidence? |

## Why ITOM sits on the CMDB

You can run Event Management without Discovery, but every alert will be an orphan. You can
run Cloud Accelerate without Discovery, but you will not know what you already own. You can
run Service Mapping without a clean CMDB, but the map will faithfully reproduce the mess.

The order that avoids rework is:

1. **Foundation data and CMDB** — people, groups, locations, CI classes, identification rules.
2. **Discovery** — populate and maintain CIs.
3. **Service Mapping** — connect CIs into service instances.
4. **AIOps** — bind events and logs to those CIs and services.
5. **Reliability** — put SLOs and ownership around the services.
6. **Cloud governance** — apply the same discipline to cloud accounts and resources.

This is a dependency order, not a law. But skipping a layer usually means rebuilding the
layer above it.

## What "agentless" and "agent-based" mean

**Agentless** means ServiceNow does not install software on the target. Discovery uses a
**MID Server** — a small ServiceNow process inside your network — to run probes over SSH,
WMI, or SNMP. The MID Server starts the connection, so you usually do not need inbound
firewall rules.

**Agent-based** means software is installed on the target. The **Agent Client Collector**
runs checks locally and sends data back through a MID Server. Use it for deep host metrics,
isolated networks, or data that agentless discovery cannot reach.

Neither is "better". They answer different constraints.

---

## What ITOM is not

- It is not a replacement for your monitoring tools. Event Management **integrates** with
  them; it does not have to replace them.
- It is not free with the platform. Most ITOM products are separate subscriptions.
- It is not a one-time project. Discovery drifts, maps drift, and alert rules rot. The
  operating model is maintenance, not installation.
- It is not magic. An alert bound to the wrong CI produces a confident, wrong answer.
