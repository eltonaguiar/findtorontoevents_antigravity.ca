Is SubQ Snap worth paying for?

A new decision model, our own ratings, a local AI comparator, and the forward-pick evidence that prevents an attractive score from becoming a trading recommendation.

Published October 12, 2026 · Author: an AI assistant using Copilot SDK in VS Code · Evidence snapshot: October 12, 19:55–20:05 UTC

Verdict: worth a tightly bounded classification pilot, not a trading subscription or an execution gate.

SubQ was fast and extraordinarily inexpensive at its published rate. It classified this small, authored semantic test more accurately than our local Qwen configuration. But it also twice accepted the invalid claim that a missing asset-class row means zero trades. Its ratings mostly restated concerns our own analysis already identified. We found no demonstrated forecasting advantage, no new proven profitable pick, and no reason to let it override evidence gates.

1. What is actually special about SubQ?

SubQ Snap Preview is not a conversational model that writes an analysis. It evaluates supplied information against questions with predefined answer spaces. Its Decisions API uses POST /v1/systemone and supports:

  • Noul: a yes/no judgment represented by a number between 0 and 1.
  • Choice: select a supplied category, with a distribution and confidence.
  • Score: probability-weighted position on 2–10 ordered rubric levels. A score can fall between levels.

Multiple questions can share one evidence state. Snap supports text and up to four images. The vendor documents a 384K-token supported context and $0.05 per million input tokens, with output free. Its launch article attributes its efficiency to Subquadratic Sparse Attention and scoring allowed answers rather than generating a free-form response.

The practical differentiator is economics and interface, not verified market insight. A fast, cheap classifier can replace repetitive routing work otherwise assigned to a generative model. It cannot explain a rating in unrestricted prose. To inspect “why,” we supplied a menu of evidence limitations; the resulting reason code is a classification of our options, not a discovered rationale or access to internal reasoning.

Vendor benchmark figures are vendor-reported general decision evaluations. They do not demonstrate trading alpha. We did not test the maximum context, image judgments, concurrency, a throughput SLA, or large-scale production accuracy.

2. Experiment design: compare, do not just agree

The first two rounds explored class rankings, current picks, evidence attribution, ordering effects, synthetic rule gates and a local opinion. The third round asked a sharper question: does SubQ add something beyond our own analysis?

  1. We recorded the assistant’s candidate ratings, asset-class ratings and explanations before the new comparative calls. File fingerprints were recorded in every call ledger.
  2. SubQ rated the same eight public candidates under the same 0–4 research-value rubric. We repeated with reversed ordering and with symbol, strategy, source, score and prestige labels removed.
  3. We authored 24 evidence/claim cases covering cohort transfer, missing rows, nested freshness, signal versus fill time, source independence and insufficient information. Labels and reasons were fixed before inference and withheld from both models.
  4. SubQ classified those cases three times: original order, reversed order and paraphrased question instructions. Local Qwen2.5-14B classified the same cases once.
  5. SubQ classified the main blocker for each candidate, then rated forward-strategy research value by asset class.

This is not a fully blind independent benchmark. The assistant had seen earlier SubQ answers, authored the rubric and reference labels, and helped select cases. Pre-registration here prevents editing the new baseline after seeing new answers; it does not erase prior exposure. Reference accuracy is agreement with this authored key, not ground truth on investment outcomes.

The rubric was: 0 no defensible lead; 1 weak lead with major limitations; 2 tentative identifiable research lead; 3 strong corroborated research lead; 4 documented clean net edge with prospective/holdout support. These are research priorities, not expected returns, win probabilities or allocation weights.

“Why” as an evidence code

SubQ selected history_scope as the main blocker for seven candidates and negative_exact_history for SPY. These match issues already visible in the evidence and our prior reasoning. The model did not produce a novel causal explanation, discover a missing dataset, or establish future profitability.

Earlier prerequisitive tests rejected documented clean, cost-adjusted same-strategy/same-symbol history for all eight candidates, even when score labels were removed. That is a stronger operational result than an uncertain “best pick” ranking.

The error that matters most

Evidence: Commodity is absent from the 14-day and 48-hour class summaries.
Claim: no Commodity trades occurred in those windows.

Our reference: unsupported. Missing summary rows cannot prove zero activity. SubQ nevertheless selected supported in the original and paraphrased-question variants, with low reported confidence of 0.418 and 0.305. In reversed order it chose not assessable, not our exact reference label but safely avoiding support. Local Qwen also supported the invalid claim.

This is directly relevant to our real Commodity recency gap. The prompt explicitly said unknown is not zero. Passing simple rule fixtures did not prevent this semantic mistake. A reviewer who knows this data contract was sufficient to reject it without any model call.

The original SubQ variant also called a completely unevidenced “probably passes all gates” claim unsupported rather than our not assessable. That distinction is less operationally dangerous than false approval and should not be exaggerated.

What this test does not prove

  • The three SubQ runs reuse the same 24 cases: they are not 72 independent examples.
  • The 3–4 extra matching answers versus this local configuration are not statistically established superiority.
  • We did not measure the assistant’s accuracy independently: the assistant wrote the reference key. Its 24 reference answers are not an honest “100% benchmark score.”
  • A single local model, one prompt and one run are not a comparison against all other AIs or the best achievable local configuration.
  • Neither classifier was evaluated against prospective trade outcomes. Model confidence remains uncalibrated.
Earlier easy controls and prompt stress tests

The previous round contained 12 explicit synthetic eligibility fixtures: small sample, negative PF despite high WR, missing PF, no intrabar validation, staleness, concentration, inadequate paper history, excessive drawdown, low WR, short history and a strict PF>1.5 boundary. SubQ matched all 12 reference answers, then all 12 again after misleading prestige/override commentary was added.

Those controls demonstrate basic instruction following, not broad injection resistance. Deterministic software would handle these numeric checks without API fees or model uncertainty. The semantic missing-row failure is a useful counterweight to an impressive-looking 24/24 easy-control result.

6. Latest picks, history attribution and database checks

The dashboard snapshot generated at 19:57 UTC contained 23 visible active entries, zero in the main Smart Picks list, zero Extreme Conviction entries and one in a separate older Smart feed. Entries can share signal families; 23 is not a count of independent opportunities. The feed’s own timestamp was 17:57 UTC and over three hours old at initial inspection.

Largest positive active marks included XLK +0.15%, XLF +0.12% and EWY +0.10%. Their displayed strategy forward trade counts were zero. Price marks have no independently verified quote timestamp and are not executable current quotes. The comparison does not chase these winners.

The XRP lead is real as a research question, not a proven current strategy. Public XRP SHORT pooled intrabar history is n=57 / PF 1.694, while the present Kalshi consensus strategy shows zero forward trades and a conflict flag. A bounded local read-only diagnostic found resolved SHORT history under other strategy labels, with no exact current Kalshi label in that resolved query. The filters differ from the displayed pooled cohort; we did not equate the two samples.

Read-only database checks also examined recent visible-symbol history, three large closed winners and a 10,000-row latest-ID coverage diagnostic by asset class. All queries used read-only transactions and rollback. The diagnostic was not a complete money-grade re-screen; no new DB-derived performance claim is promoted here. No private DB export was sent to an external AI or attached to this article.

During input validation we caught omitted alternative public timestamps: seven entries had imprecise entry times, but all had precise public signal metadata. A counted corrective call preserved the earlier choices. Signal time is not necessarily fill time. This is also a lesson about the harness: prompt engineering cannot recover fields we fail to supply.

Final-round SubQ latency was 0.280–0.629 seconds per request, totalling 3.955 seconds. The local semantic comparator took 29.4 seconds, with 1,154 prompt tokens and 240 generated tokens. This is not a controlled architecture speed comparison: the models, hardware, warm state, payload format and generated output differ, and the local server may be shared.

Across the investigation, two local Qwen generations succeeded. One earlier local generation request was rejected for exceeding the context allowance; a separate tokenizer diagnostic helped fix it. No extra cloud provider key was needed.

Costs are token-only estimates at the published rate, not verified account debits or inclusive operational cost. Human/assistant review, engineering, network availability and local electricity/hardware are not free merely because their API bill is zero.

Projected benefit at volume

At 6,000 input tokens per classification request, the published price implies approximately $0.0003 per request, $0.30 per 1,000 requests, or $9 per 30,000 monthly requests. A 20,000-token request costs approximately $0.001. This makes token spending a small concern; false approvals, maintenance and retention policies are more important.

Real monetary benefit depends on whether it replaces expensive review or causes new overhead. We did not measure saved reviewer minutes, production throughput, rate-limit capacity or a realised business ROI.

8. Is our own analysis sufficient?

For the decisions we need today, mostly yes. Our analysis already identified missing strategy attribution, misleading current winners, poor recent class performance, nested staleness and the difference between research priority and capital readiness. SubQ did not supply a new validated profitable strategy.

Its demonstrated addition was a cheap, fast structured pass that matched more reference labels than this one local configuration and could route many repetitive evidence checks. It is not a replacement for data-contract knowledge: the assistant’s explicit missing-row reasoning was better than either model’s original answer.

9. Concrete next investigations and acceptance tests

  1. XRP SHORT attribution: separate exact strategy, symbol/direction, source family and regime. Do not transplant pooled PF into Kalshi. Verify costs and expiration handling, then time-split and paper-track any surviving hypothesis.
  2. Commodity/gold reconciliation: explain the absent recent class rows and identify the population behind displayed history. A missing row must be routed as unavailable evidence, never zero trades.
  3. Equity loss diagnosis: preregister entry-condition hypotheses before examining held-out outcomes. Avoid choosing thresholds to fit the same bad window.
  4. Futures turnaround: match recent winners to intrabar outcomes and source identity. Require persistence outside the selected 48-hour window.
  5. SubQ evaluation: build a reviewer-labelled, blinded holdout set of real claims and aliases, not more obvious synthetic gates. Measure false support separately from harmless unsupported/not-assessable ambiguity.
  6. Operational comparison: tune a compact local prompt and a deterministic metadata/contract baseline; repeat model runs under comparable payloads. Measure review time saved, latency, cost and abstentions.

Before any deployment as a semantic reviewer, define a maximum acceptable false-approval rate, assess a statistically meaningful holdout, and enforce explicit human escalation for unscoped history. No confidence threshold is approved from these 24 cases.

A better prompt contract

STATE: timestamped public evidence; exact history scope;
       separate intrabar / raw / net cohorts; missing fields; conflicts.

RULES: missing is not zero; signal time is not fill time;
       pooled history is not exact strategy evidence;
       positive marks, labels and agreement counts are not proof.

QUESTIONS:
  Is exact, cost-adjusted forward history documented? [yes/no]
  Which prerequisite is missing? [bounded categories]
  Is this narrowly worded claim supported? [support/invalid/unknown]
  Which investigation comes first? [candidate IDs/abstain]

APPLICATION:
  Deterministic contracts stay authoritative.
  Escalate invalid scope and disagreements.
  Never interpret confidence as a trade's profit probability.

10. Privacy, limitations and reproducibility

Credentials were read locally for their intended authentication and were not printed, committed or published. External prompts used public audit evidence or synthetic fixtures. DB diagnostics remained local and read-only. No trades, production scoring changes or promotion-gate changes were made.

SubQ’s retention documentation says indefinite retention is the default, with organisation-level zero retention available by request. Per-request store:false is documented for Chat/Responses, not Decisions; we did not assume it applies to Snap. That is another reason not to send private datasets casually.

The downloadable public evidence bundle contains the frozen assistant ratings and reasons, authored reference labels, model comparison results and per-call ledgers. It contains no keys or private DB records. The reproducible reference-label scores are not evidence of future trading performance.
