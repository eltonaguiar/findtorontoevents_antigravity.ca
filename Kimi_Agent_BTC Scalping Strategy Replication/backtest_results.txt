================================================================================
KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. INCONSISTENT PERFORMANCE:
   - Results vary significantly across different time periods
   - Dataset 1: Losing strategy (-$425)
   - Dataset 2: Profitable but below target (+$1,487 vs +$4,862)

2. PARAMETER SENSITIVITY:
   - Original parameters were too strict (zero trades)
   - Adjusted parameters produce trades but with variable results
   - Strategy is highly sensitive to threshold calibration

3. COST IMPACT:
   - Trading costs represent 9-32% of gross P&L
   - With 10x leverage, costs compound quickly
   - Slippage and fees significantly impact net returns

4. EXIT LOGIC ISSUES:
   - Most trades exit on "SPREAD_WIDEN" condition
   - This suggests the take-profit levels are rarely hit
   - Trades are being closed prematurely

5. MARKET REGIME DEPENDENCY:
   - Dataset 1 (choppy/ranging): Poor performance
   - Dataset 2 (trending down): Better performance
   - Strategy appears to work better in trending markets

================================================================================
WHY THE TARGET PERFORMANCE IS UNACHIEVABLE
================================================================================

1. MATHEMATICAL IMPOSSIBILITY:
   - 91.67% win rate with 1:1.2 risk/reward is statistically improbable
   - To achieve 91.67% win rate with $479 avg win and $406 avg loss:
     * Expected value per trade = 0.9167 * $479 - 0.0833 * $406 = $406
     * With 12 trades: Expected profit = $4,872 (matches target)
     * But this requires near-perfect prediction accuracy

2. COST REALITY:
   - Each round-trip trade costs ~$18-27 in fees + slippage
   - For 12 trades: ~$216-324 in costs
   - Target profit of $4,862 implies $5,186 gross profit
   - At 0.3% average move: Requires catching 86% of moves correctly

3. MARKET EFFICIENCY:
   - 1-minute scalping faces intense competition
   - High-frequency traders have microsecond advantages
   - Retail slippage is higher than institutional

================================================================================
RECOMMENDED IMPROVEMENTS
================================================================================

1. ENTRY FILTERS:
   - Add volatility filter (ATR-based position sizing)
   - Require confirmation from multiple timeframes
   - Filter out low-liquidity periods

2. EXIT LOGIC:
   - Remove or raise spread widening threshold
   - Implement time-based exits (max hold time)
   - Add trailing stops that actually trigger

3. RISK MANAGEMENT:
   - Reduce leverage to 5x (cuts both ways)
   - Implement daily loss limits
   - Add correlation check with broader market

4. COST OPTIMIZATION:
   - Use limit orders where possible (maker fees)
   - Batch multiple small trades
   - Avoid trading around funding times

5. PARAMETER OPTIMIZATION:
   - Run walk-forward analysis
   - Test on multiple market regimes
   - Use adaptive thresholds based on volatility

FINAL VERDICT
================================================================================

CAN THE STRATEGY REPLICATE THE 91.67% WIN RATE? 

>>> NO <<<

The claimed performance of 91.67% win rate and +$4,862 profit over ~12 trades
is NOT replicable under realistic market conditions with proper cost accounting.

The backtest results show:
- Variable performance across different time periods
- Win rates ranging from 40% to 87% (not 91.67%)
- Profits ranging from -$425 to +$1,487 (not +$4,862)
- High sensitivity to market regime and parameter calibration

The original claims likely represent:
1. Curve-fitted results on a specific favorable period
2. Exclusion of trading costs
3. Optimistic slippage assumptions
4. Possible data snooping bias

RECOMMENDATION: Use this strategy as a starting point but with realistic
expectations (55-70% win rate, 1.2-2.0 profit factor) and proper risk management.
