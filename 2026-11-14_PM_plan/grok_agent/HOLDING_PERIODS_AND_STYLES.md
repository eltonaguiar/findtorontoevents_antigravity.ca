# Holding Periods, Trading Styles, and Famous Systems

## Style Comparison (Grounded Reality)

| Style          | Typical Hold     | Cost Sensitivity | Edge Difficulty | Historical Reality for Retail/Systematic |
|----------------|------------------|------------------|-----------------|------------------------------------------|
| Long-term / Buy-and-hold | Years          | Low              | Low (beta)      | Highest probability of positive real returns via equity risk premium |
| Swing          | Days–weeks      | Medium           | Medium          | Possible but requires strong regime filters and cost control |
| Day trading    | Intraday         | High             | Very high       | Majority of participants lose after costs |
| Scalping       | Seconds–minutes  | Extreme          | Extreme         | Almost always loses after realistic spreads/slippage for non-HFT |

Day trading and scalping look attractive in simulations until costs, slippage, and multiple-testing penalties are applied. Famous contest winners often use aggressive paper-trading behavior that does not translate to real money.

## Famous / Contest-Related Systems

### Rob Hoffman Inventory Retracement Bar
- Widely discussed on YouTube and TradingView communities.
- Multiple independent testers have reported ~60% win rates and positive PF on short samples (BTC, Solana, etc.) with 1:1.5 R:R.
- Hoffman has won real-money competitions in Europe using disciplined rule-following.
- Caveat: short-sample YouTube backtests are not proof. Requires multi-year, multi-asset, cost-adjusted walk-forward verification before any confidence. Common with many retail systems.

### TradingView "The Leap" Contests
- Paper trading competitions with large participant pools.
- Winners often show high returns in short windows (30 days).
- Common patterns: aggressive sizing, trend or Elliott Wave ideas, awareness of fees.
- Paper results systematically overstate real-money performance (no psychological pressure, sometimes different fills).

### Classic Academic / Practitioner Systems with Longer Track Records
- **Time-series momentum / CTA trend-following**: decades of evidence across commodities, equities, bonds, FX. Low correlation to traditional assets. One of the few active strategies with persistent published support.
- **Cross-sectional momentum** (12-1 month): strong historical equity evidence; suffers in certain regimes.
- **Dual momentum** (Gary Antonacci): absolute + relative. Simple, published, and has multi-year real track records in various implementations.
- **Quality + momentum** combinations: used by several large quant managers.
- **Low-volatility anomaly**: historically strong risk-adjusted returns; can lag in strong bull markets.

## Practical Recommendation by Style
- **Core capital**: long-term low-cost index exposure.
- **Research priority for alpha**: medium-term swing / dual-momentum style on equities and commodities (lower cost friction than pure day trading).
- **Avoid** pure scalping and high-frequency day trading until the system has demonstrated clean edge on slower timeframes.

Fact-check any specific contest claim or YouTube result with SubQ or primary sources before treating it as evidence.
