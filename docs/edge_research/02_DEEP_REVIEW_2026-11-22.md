# Deep review of audit2 / audit3 — 2026-11-22

Verification, not advocacy. Every number below was read from disk in this session; where the brief that
commissioned this review disagreed with disk, the disagreement is stated in-line and disk wins. Sources and
line-level provenance are in §7.

## 1. The question asked

The question was two-part: **which investment vehicle is actually best for a short horizon versus a long
horizon** (GIC/savings, broad ETF, individual stocks, crypto, futures, forex), and **whether this repo's own
audited strategies justify putting capital behind them**. The first part is answerable with measured market
series and a break-even calculation (§4). The second part is answerable only by reading the audit surfaces
honestly, and the honest reading is that they do not justify capital: the ledger's headline verdict is
**0 PROVEN, 0 CANDIDATE** (`audit_dashboard/data/audit3_ledger.json`) and two separate internal claims
(the population it scored, and the Sharpe of its one positive family) fail independent checks (§2, §3). One
strategy family was tested independently and was **KILLed** (§3). So the answer to "should I allocate on this
evidence" is no, and the answer to "what should I hold" is a plain broad equity ETF for the long horizon and a
GIC/T-bill for the short one (§4).

## 3. The D7 detail — why "positive mean return" was not edge

The breakout test (N=20 and N=55 lookbacks, 5 bp/side, 1× and 2×) produced a genuinely positive mean:

| | N=20 (390 trades) | N=55 (268 trades) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean net / trade @1× | **+1.117%** | **+1.271%** |
| 95% bootstrap CI | **[+0.251%, +2.035%]** | **[+0.288%, +2.408%]** |
| Mean net @2× | +1.017% | +1.171% |
| Median net | **−1.450%** | −1.531% |
| Win rate | 38.5% | 39.6% |
| Positive walk-forward folds | 3 / 5 | 4 / 5 |

Both means stay positive at 2× cost, and both have a CI excluding zero. Those are the first three gates,
and they pass. Two gates fail, and they are the ones that matter:

1. **The random-entry null.** Fifty hundred shuffles of entry dates on the *same* symbols with the *same*
   exit and stop logic earned a null mean net of **+0.715% (N=20) / +0.815% (N=55) per trade**, with null
   Sharpe mean 0.359 / 0.411. In other words, *a random long entry in this window already made +0.7–0.8% per
   trade.* The rule's +1.1–1.3% is an increment, but the observed Sharpe is only at the ~70th percentile of
   that null, not above its 95th. The rule is not distinguishable from being long.
2. **Selection alpha.** Alpha = pick net minus direction-adjusted buy-and-hold over the same entry→exit
   window. It is **negative** at both lookbacks (point −0.123% / −0.110%), and its CI upper bound barely
   crosses zero at 1× while the lower bound is firmly negative at 2× (−0.385% / −0.396%). The rule **lost to
   simply holding the same ETFs** over the same windows.

Add the shape of the distribution: mean +1.12% but **median −1.45%**, win rate ~39%, trade-sequence max
drawdown −78.7%. The mean is carried by a handful of large winners on top of a long-biased exposure. The
ledger's claimed +0.61 Sharpe on 33 folds did not reproduce (0.45–0.51 on 5 folds), and even the claimed
value would have sat near the null's p95 at best.

**The lesson, stated plainly:** in a rising market a long-biased rule looks profitable while doing worse
than buy-and-hold. A positive mean is not edge until it beats a random-entry null *and* earns positive
selection alpha. This one beat neither.

## 4. The horizon answer, with measured numbers

Common window 2021-11-22 → 2026-11-19 (5 years; BTC to 2026-11-20). Vol and drawdown are annualised from
daily bars.

| Vehicle | Horizon | Measured | Vol | Max DD | Investment or trade? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GIC / HISA | 0–3 yrs | known nominal rate, no market series in repo | ~0 | ~0 | **investment (floor)** |
| Broad equity ETF (SPY) | 5+ yrs | **+10.4%/yr, Sharpe 0.66** | 17.4% | −24.5% | **investment** |
| Bitcoin (BTC) | any | **+1.3%/yr**, Sharpe 0.24 | 42.1% | **−72.7%** | trade |
| FX spot | any | **not a return** without carry (EURUSD −4.0%, USDJPY +44.3% price change) | 7–9% | — | not an investment |
| Futures | — | **n=4** in the ledger, insufficient | — | — | trade |
| All pods | — | **0 forward rows** for the only pre-registered pod (POD-EQUITY-BREAKOUT), 0 for all pods in `sidecar_promotion_status` | — | — | proven nothing |
| Breakout rule (this test) | — | +1.12%/trade but **alpha −0.285%** (CI-LB) | 32.4% | **−78.7%** | **trade, and a losing one vs holding** |

For contrast on the same window: QQQ +10.8%/yr (vol 23.4%, DD −35.1%), GLD +14.8%/yr (vol 19.3%),
IWM +4.0%/yr, TLT **−8.1%/yr**.

**The GIC break-even.** A risky asset must earn enough *arithmetic* return to match a GIC after volatility
drag (0.5·σ²) and cost drag. Against a **4% GIC with 0.5% cost**:

- 15% vol → **5.6%/yr** needed
- 30% vol → **9.0%/yr** needed
- 60% vol → **22.5%/yr** needed

(At 0% cost the figures are 5.1% / 8.5% / 22.0%.) SPY's +10.4% at 17% vol clears a 4% GIC comfortably. BTC's
+1.3% at 42% vol needs roughly 13%/yr to break even and delivers a tenth of that, so it loses to any GIC once
volatility drag is counted. For the short horizon, a GIC or T-bill at ~0 vol beats anything in this table on
risk-adjusted terms. **For the long horizon, a held broad equity ETF is the only vehicle here with an
investment-grade profile; everything else is a trade.**

## 5. The three highest-value fixes the evidence supports

1. **POD-EQUITY-BREAKOUT emits zero rows.** Its four strategies never produced a single row in
   `trading_picks` or `at_signal_outcomes`; [`tools/pod_emission_audit.py`](../../tools/pod_emission_audit.py:4)
   records it: *"15 of 23 registered pod strategies had zero rows … including all four of
   POD-EQUITY-BREAKOUT."* It is the only `pre_registered` pod, and therefore the only pod that could ever
   mint a PROVEN_T2 verdict on pre-committed evidence, and it produces nothing. Honest caveat: §3 suggests
   that even once emitting, this family would probably be KILLed on the random-entry and selection-alpha
   gates. That is still worth knowing, and worth knowing cheaply, rather than by chasing a phantom emitter
   for months.
2. **H-119 has 430 forward rows but no pod owner.** `reports/forward_observations.jsonl` holds 430 of its
   432 rows tagged `H-119` (the other two are `H-2026-11-22-*`), and
   `reports/hypothesis_registry.json` lists it `status: FORWARD_ACCRUING` with an acceptance gate at
   `min_n: 150`. The rows keep arriving, but no entry in `audit_dashboard/data/audit2_pods.json` (0 matches)
   or in `audit3_confirmatory.json` (absent from the repo; only stale copies in worktrees) claims it. Rows
   with no owner can never promote anything: the data is real and the wiring discards it.
3. **audit3 is not reproducible from a committed file.** `audit3_ledger.json` declares
   `source: /tmp/audit3/dashboard_data.json` and `provenance.source_file: /tmp/audit3/dashboard_data.json`,
   and its `canonical_rows_sha256` (`63609da5…`) commits to rows that are not committed anywhere. Until that
   rows file (or the payload that carries it) is committed, every verdict on the audit3 surface is a claim,
   not evidence.

## 6. Standing honesty rules for this programme

Carrying these forward, because each one was learned the hard way in this review:

- **`UNMEASURABLE` is a legitimate result.** No GIC series exists in the repo, so the GIC row is a
  break-even sensitivity, not a fabricated return series. Say so and stop.
- **A positive mean is not edge** until it beats a random-entry null *and* earns positive selection alpha
  over buy-and-hold.
- **Never launder a truncated sample into a full-book number.** A 3,500-row recency slice is not the
  8,827-row population, and neither is the 8,769-row one.
- **When two surfaces disagree, report the disagreement** rather than picking the flattering one.
- **Selection on the test statistic is a failure, not a win.** Choosing a 42-day window after seeing it pass
  is the same error as choosing N=20 after seeing N=20 win; both lookbacks are reported here for that reason.
