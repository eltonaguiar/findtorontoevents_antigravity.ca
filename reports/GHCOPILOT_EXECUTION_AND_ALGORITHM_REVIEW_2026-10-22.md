# Algorithm review: realistic exits, horizons and uncertainty

Goal #1. Research, not investment advice. Snapshot-specific findings, not current
orders or a prediction that a security will reach its target.

## Decision

**No reviewed strategy is independently MONEY-READY.** A public research product
can disclose this honestly; a public launch does not make an investment book safe.
Mutual funds can lose principal, and past fund holdings are not trading signals.
No calibrated probability of future profit or price forecast is established here.

The immediate work is verification and sealed paper observation, not adding
another hundred strategies to an unreliable outcome ledger. Do not discard a
strategy merely because it fails this proof: missing evidence and a disproven
economic edge are different. No new strategy kills are authorized by this report.

## What does a pick actually mean?

A complete prospective plan needs:

1. Exact source, strategy revision, instrument/venue and LONG or SHORT.
2. First-observed UTC time, causal features and an entry rule that can really fill.
3. Entry reference, take-profit (TP), stop-loss (SL), gap/slippage assumptions.
4. **Maximum holding time and a time-stop rule**, not merely a chart timeframe.
5. Position size, concurrent-position limits, costs and market-session calendar.
6. Probabilities calibrated for these exact exit rules and this horizon on unseen
   observations; otherwise certainty is **unverified**, not a fabricated percentage.
7. A benchmark and a daily marked-to-market equity curve for the same exposure.

**ELI5:** A pick is a testable plan: when to start, which way it bets, when it
wins, when it gives up, and how long it waits. A target is a planned exit, not a
promise. A stop is not a guarantee against a worse fill.

## Stronger double-checks: prioritized and falsifiable

These are validation tasks, not claims that all tests have now been executed.
The executable scorecard records unavailable evidence as N/A.
The source of the pass thresholds remains
[Grok's published gates](https://github.com/eltonaguiar/COMMUNITY/blob/main/GROK_20261022_1855.MD).
No new research test may be tuned and then described as untouched OOS proof.

| Check | ELI5 / why bother | Required input / honest failure condition |
|---|---|---|
| Event-time replay and first touch | Did the stop happen before the claimed win? | Independent fine-grained OHLC/ticks, session calendars, adjusted prices and original observation times; ambiguous bars use stop-first or remain unresolved |
| Purged temporal validation plus embargo | Do not let tomorrow's answers leak into today's training | Purge training labels whose information intervals overlap test labels; embargo chosen prospectively from the information horizon, not tuned after outcomes |
| Matched random-entry and random-direction controls | Is timing/direction better than doing something random under the same rules? | Same instrument, sessions, horizon, TP/SL, exposure, costs and availability; random controls must face the same fill constraints |
| Day/week blocks, dependence and simultaneous exposure | Twenty clones on one market move are not twenty independent discoveries | Whole-day/week and portfolio-block resampling; report effective data support and stress both clustering choices |
| DSR, PBO and family-level multiplicity | How many losing experiments were tried before showing the winner? | Complete aligned trial ledger and all variants; missing trials mean N/A, not a guessed trial count |
| Nested chronological calibration | Does an "80%" forecast succeed near 80% on untouched future cases? | Separate training, calibration and final test periods, proper scoring rules, reliability bins with intervals and drift monitoring |
| Brier/log score versus a base-rate model | Is the fancy probability better than a simple honest guess? | Compare unseen predictions to an ex-ante rolling base rate; do not fit/calibrate/evaluate on the same rows |
| Competing exit / time-to-event analysis | How likely is TP before SL, and how long does that take? | Prospective entry, TP/SL/time-stop, gap fills and censored/unresolved observations; never remove still-open losers to flatter WR |
| Funded daily MTM and benchmark decomposition | Did one wallet actually grow more safely than passive exposure? | Position sizing, cash, concurrent positions, total returns, fees/funding/borrow/roll; calculate Sharpe/Sortino/Calmar/CVaR only on that book |
| Parameter-neighborhood and regime stability | A real effect should not disappear when one knob moves slightly | Preregistered perturbations and an untouched final interval; discovery sensitivity is not another OOS test |
| Capacity / impact / tail stress | Would the result survive realistic size and a bad gap? | Participation, spread, borrow, contract rolls, liquidity and funding stress; no institutional-size extrapolation from normalized percentages |

Distinct closing days and entry-day bootstrap clusters are different quantities;
both are displayed/disclosed. A bootstrap confidence interval measures sampling
uncertainty conditional on the observed sample, not the chance a future trade wins.

### Holding horizons must define separate experiments

| Research question | Horizon to specify before testing | Essential extra check |
|---|---|---|
| Scalping | Exact minutes and session cutoff | Tick/spread/fill realism; bar-close prices are inadequate |
| Intraday | Exact session end and overnight rule | Exchange calendar, halt/gap policy, latency |
| Swing | Exact maximum sessions or hours, not the word SWING | Overnight/weekend gaps, funding/borrow and a time stop |
| Tactical ETF allocation | Exact rebalance schedule and trend/risk rule | Fresh adjusted total returns, dividends, risk-matched passive benchmark |
| Fundamental stock/fund research | Explicit quarters/years and rebalance rule | Point-in-time filings, report lag, delistings and total-return benchmarks |

No particular duration in the last table is a recommendation or an empirically
optimal hold. The only implemented experiment duration described here is the
existing paper monitor's 48 hours. New backtests require the repository's
hypothesis preregistration before evaluation.

Read-only inspected artifacts at the isolated branch's base, not a live retrain:

  recent closed rows; 32 features. Fold AUCs approximately
  0.592, 0.487, 0.532, 0.589, 0.608. These are mixed discrimination results,
  not return forecasts.
- Fold Brier scores approximately 0.270, 0.290, 0.254, 0.241, 0.238.
  Descriptive constant-fold-prevalence reference scores are approximately
  0.248, 0.243, 0.247, 0.246, 0.250: the first three folds are worse.
  That reference uses each held-out fold's realized prevalence and is **not**
  a deployable ex-ante baseline. A new validation must use a prior-only base rate.

  isotonic fitting. This is useful prior art, but this path does not establish
  purged event-interval validation plus a separately untouched calibration test.
- The older generic calibration routine fits Platt/isotonic and compares their
  ECE **on the same labels used to fit them**. Its April-14 report's very small
  calibrated ECE is in-sample, not a certified probability for an October pick.
- ML health artifact at 2026-10-22T18:59:56Z reports
  `ml_trading_enabled:false`, multiplier 0, feature coverage 0%, and HALTED.
  This artifact is not proof every production caller enforces that halt.
- The gatekeeper A/B workflow has recent successful runs. The source comments
  name four leakage-prone features and separate OLD/NEW arms.
  A green training job proves execution, not profitable or unbiased labels.

Do not retrain on corrupt closes and expect the model to fix the corruption.
First establish clean causal labels, replay and timestamps, then test ranking
and calibrated risk estimates in a shadow book against simple baselines.
Do not replace a 48-hour model with a months-long holding claim without
relabeling, preregistering and independently validating that different problem.

## Reconciliation with Grok's latest protocol

The analogies are useful, but these distinctions prevent another misleading
scoreboard:

- "Crypto loses half a percent before fees" is a claim about a particular
  dataset, not a fact established for every crypto strategy here. Our filtered,
  capped snapshot has crypto scenario PF 0.91; it does not certify that exact
  average or the full database.
- A PF bootstrap samples whole days **with replacement**. Merely rearranging
  the same trades cannot change PF. Day clusters preserve some shared shocks,
  but adjacent-day dependence may require longer blocks.
- One hundred trades is a screening minimum, not proof. Nine independent days
  are still nine days even if they contain hundreds of trades.
- DSR is not a probability the next trade wins. It discounts a Sharpe estimate
  for the search across strategies; trial count, return frequency, skew and
  kurtosis matter. A result outside [0, 1] is not this probability-scale statistic.
- Slower trading can reduce turnover costs, but does not guarantee alpha.
  Claims that every scalper loses or every successful firm uses slow strategies
  are not established by this bounded investigation.
- Ten years helps cover market regimes; it is not available for every asset.
  Do not fabricate pre-inception crypto history, splice contracts without a
  roll/collateral policy, or treat adjusted index prices as executable fills.

## Follow-up: Grok's 19:30 winners note

Read from COMMUNITY/main `GROK_20261022_1930_WINNERS.MD`; claims below are
cross-checks of its published table and code excerpt, not a reproduced backtest.

1. **Seven CANDIDATE labels do not establish our common CANDIDATE contract.**
   Its SPY trend row has n=16, MDD=26% and MC percentile=84. Its sector momentum
   row has MDD=20.9% and DSR=0.75. RSI2 has DSR=0.31. The stated strict gates
   cannot be waived because a strategy looks promising. These may be informal
   research leads; do not import their labels into the verified panel without
   the required hard-gate, null-test and sealed-forward evidence.
2. **Daily averaging of closing-trade returns is still not an account curve.**
   The proposed `compoundDailyEwPct` avoids sequentially staking the whole book
   on each overlapping trade, but assigns capital retrospectively according to
   which trades close that day. It omits open-position daily marks, idle capital,
   actual sizing, financing and exposure constraints. Consequently the claimed
   -13.3% / +147.7% are not verified funded returns. Do not substitute this patch
   for the current explicit "unverified" compound headline. Its diff also edits
   generated `audit_dashboard/index.html`, not the canonical template.
3. **MC percentile needs a reproducible null.** The reported 91.5, 93 and 100
   values are not independently reproduced here. Name the statistic, gate,
   simulation count, seed, allowed dates, symbol/exposure constraints, costs,
   exits and tie convention. With N random runs, a valid finite-simulation
   upper-tail estimate is `(1 + count(null >= observed)) / (N + 1)`;
   beating every simulated run does not mean zero false-positive probability.
   Failure means the entry advantage was not demonstrated, not that none exists.
4. **Related algorithms already exist, but are not interchangeable.**
   `alpha_engine/connors_rsi2.py` documents RSI2<5, RSI2>65 exit or ten-day
   maximum, whereas the new claim is RSI2<10 and exit above SMA5. Its universe
   includes SPY, QQQ and IWM. An average four-day holding period is not a hard
   four-day deadline. The exact five-ETF tested set is not supplied in this note.
5. **Sector rotation also has multiple existing versions.**
   `tools/backtest_etf_sector_rotation.py` uses eleven sectors:
   XLF, XLE, XLK, XLV, XLI, XLY, XLP, XLU, XLB, XLRE and XLC; monthly
   12-1 momentum, top three, optionally short bottom three. The claimed nine-
   sector SPY-filtered variant is not thereby reproduced. The separate
   `tools/run_etf_sector_rotation.py` is an opt-in, default-OFF sidecar with a
   60-day default lookback; existence is not proof of live wiring or parity.
6. **Actual page visibility:** the frozen published payload contains one open
   related record: KBE, source `etf_sector_rotation`, strategy
   `etf_rsi2_pullback`. This proves that related record is published, not that
   it implements the newly claimed test. Search the main page's active table
   for that exact symbol/source/strategy. It is a dated observation, not a
   verified current buy. No fresh October-22-close basket was computed here.
7. **Fund claims need their own witnesses.** Canadian fee availability, fund
   returns, crash recovery and "none of 59 beat 60/40" cannot be established
   from this winners note. The quoted mixed book has lower CAGR but higher
   reported Sharpe than SPY; "beat" must specify return, risk and exposure.
   Neither index funds nor balanced ETFs are guaranteed safe.

The fee example is a deterministic illustration, not a fund forecast:
`10000 * 1.07**20` is approximately $38,697;
`10000 * 1.068**20` is approximately $37,276;
`10000 * 1.05**20` is approximately $26,533. Under this simplified
return-minus-fee convention the zero-fee case pays no fund fee. The differences
include both fees and their lost compounding, not an upfront bill. Taxes,
tracking difference, trading fees and variable returns are omitted.
