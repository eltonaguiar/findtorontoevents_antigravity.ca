## Why this matters more than it looks

H-236's `REFUTED` on 44 names was read as "the edge is universe-dependent — it only works on the 10
mega caps." That reading was wrong. Over ten years the *broad* universe produces **+7.80 bp/day
CI-LB**, almost the same as the 10-name book's +7.246 over fifteen years. The 44-name failure was a
**short-sample artifact of a 5-year window**, exactly what H-241 predicted when it measured the CI
half-width at 12.673 bp against a 1.27 bp delisting stress.

So the ordering of the three problems, corrected:

1. **Sampling error** — binding; fixed only by more history (done here).
2. **Multiplicity** — still binding; N just rose by one and nothing has been re-deflated.
3. **Survivorship** — not binding (H-241: 10% of the interval).

## What this does NOT do

- **It does not make H-201 investable.** H-201's own cell still fails DSR at 0.9469 vs 0.95 on N=29;
  H-242 adds a trial, moving the bar in the *wrong* direction. The DSR for H-242 has not been
  computed, and until it is, this is a replication with a pass on the CI gate only — not on the
  multiplicity gate.
- **It does not clear the survivorship flag.** The universe is still today's 44 names. Ten years of
  survivors is still survivors. Flag remains `BOUNDED`.
- **It does not license the 14-year cell.** The cache happens to start 2012-01-03, which is *more*
  history than registered. That window is reported for context only; substituting it for the
  registered 10-year window after seeing the cache is precisely the substitution pre-registration
  exists to prevent.
- Cost is charged at 10 bp/side (20 bp round trip), while the reconciled equity cost of record is
  **4.01 bp round trip** (n=191). The result is therefore *pessimistic on cost* — which is the safe
  direction, and it still fails on multiplicity, not on cost.
