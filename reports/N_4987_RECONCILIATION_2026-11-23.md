## 2. Pricing N = 4,987 on the same basis as the existing grid

Formula: expected maximum Sharpe under the null (Bailey–López de Prado),

$$\mathrm{SR}^{*} = \sigma_{sr}\left[(1-\gamma)\,\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{N}\right) + \gamma\,\Phi^{-1}\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{Ne}\right)\right]$$

with $\sigma_{sr} = 0.6488$ and $\gamma = 0.5772156649$.

## 4. The honest position on kilo's number

**Kilo is not wrong to use 4,987, and I am not wrong to use 120.** They answer different questions:

- **120 answers "how many hypotheses did we commit to testing?"** It is fixed *before* the results,
  which is the entire point of pre-registration (M-107). A denominator chosen after seeing 0/6 is a
  decision, not a measurement.
- **4,987 answers "how much searching happened?"** It is the true cost of the search and it is much
  larger than the registry admits. If the incubator really generated 4,987 permutations, then the
  **registry understates the trial debt by ~40×**, and every hurdle in the estate computed from the
  registry is too low.

That second reading is the more damaging one and it favours kilo's conclusion. I therefore adopt it as
a **reported bound**, not as the primary:

- **Primary:** registry count at time of test (127 today) — because pre-registered.
- **Bound:** **4,987** (incubator permutations) — because it is the true search cost.
- **Not used:** ~25 (freebuff's effective count) — estimated from the realised trial returns, so the
  denominator can be shrunk by the outcome being deflated.

## 5. Consequence recorded for the registry

The 5 strategies pre-registered today (H-243…H-247, from the hub's "Deploy These" list) inherit
