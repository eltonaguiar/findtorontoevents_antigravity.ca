Doc reviewed: `reports/quant_swarm_merged_round2_2026-05-12.md`.

## 1. OVER-weight — data-driven CT=F enthusiasm without regime conditioning

The merge anoints COMMODITY (CT=F) as "unanimous, highest-conviction edge"
(§"STRONG CONVERGENCE #1", lines 13-24) on PF 1.78 / n=750 alone. From
the All Weather lens this is over-weighted: cotton's edge is **path-
dependent on the inflation/growth quadrant**. The merge cites Miffre 2010
class-wide alpha but does not condition the 1-contract go-live on the
current quadrant (rising-growth / falling-inflation kills commodity carry).
Promote-to-LIVE should be gated on quadrant, not on a 4-week paper window.

## 2. UNDER-weight — no macro/quadrant exposure budget anywhere in the plan

The Month-6 capital envelope (lines 192-204) allocates by **asset class**,
not by **economic environment**. There is no row for "rising-growth sleeve
vs falling-growth sleeve" or "inflation-up vs inflation-down". That is the
core All Weather principle and it is absent. Bridgewater Week-1 priority
("HMM states into emission gating", line 88) is listed but the merged
allocation table never translates HMM regime into a sizing constraint.

## 3. BLIND SPOT across all 7 personas — data-fit edges, not principle-driven exposures

Every persona — Renaissance, Two Sigma, DE Shaw, Citadel, AQR, Bridgewater,
and the merge itself — frames the problem as "find / validate / de-leak
**signals**." None ask the prior question: *what economic exposure are we
intentionally buying?* The swarm is reverse-engineering edge from historical
trade tape (strat_fwd_wr leakage is the symptom, not the disease). A
principle-driven fund starts with "we want long-inflation, short-growth
exposure this quarter" and only then asks which instrument cheapest expresses
it. The system has no environmental-bias register, no quadrant-conditioned
target exposure, no "what would we hold if all our models broke?" baseline.
Until that is built, every CPCV/PBO/DSR gate is just better-disciplined
curve-fitting on the same regime sample.
