# R4b can refute but cannot confirm — its CONFIRM gate needs 13.2%/yr

**Date:** 2026-11-23 · **Owner:** buffy (freebuff) · **Paper only, zero capital, not advice**
**Reproduce:** `python3 tools/r4b_power.py` (or `--json`)

## The question nobody asked before the clock started

R4b is pre-registered, self-driving, and its first legitimate record is 2026-11-30. Before that
record exists, one question can still change the design: **can 60 monthly observations detect an
edge of the size we would actually care about?**

`docs/preregistration/ETF_TREND_FORWARD_2026-11-23.md` §7 says:

> | Earliest possible CONFIRM | ~**60 months** | the interval can finally be tight enough |

and justifies it at line 97 with `≈ 10%/√n years. To get a standard error of 4.32% you need
n ≈ 5.4 years`. That reasoning stops one step short, and the missing step is the whole answer.

## What I did, and what I refused to do

I did **not** run the momentum rule over history. Doing so would consume a trial, and this family's
entire value is that its trial count is 1. I read only the **universe's monthly volatility** — a
market-data statistic, not a return series of the rule.

Universe: the frozen 20 ETFs, **61 monthly observations** each (5y cache, 2021-11 → 2026-11).

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| mean single-ETF monthly σ | **4.50%** (15.6%/yr) |
| top-5 equal-weight book σ, ρ=0.3 | 2.99%/mo (10.4%/yr) |
| **book σ, ρ=0.5 (base case)** | **3.49%/mo (12.1%/yr)** |
| book σ, ρ=0.7 | 3.92%/mo (13.6%/yr) |
| monthly hurdle (4.32%/yr) | **36.0 bp** |

ρ is assumed, not measured, because measuring the rule's own pairwise correlation means running
it. Three values are reported so the conclusion can be checked for sensitivity to that assumption;
it survives all three.

## The arithmetic

At n=60 the standard error of the mean monthly excess is **45.0 bp** (5.4%/yr). The frozen CONFIRM
rule is *one-sided 95% lower bound > 4.32%*, so the point estimate must clear

```
36.0 bp + 1.645 × 45.0 bp = 110.0 bp/month  =  13.2%/yr
```

That is the bar. Now the power to clear it:

| true monthly edge | equivalent | power to CONFIRM at n=60 |
|---|---|---|
| 36 bp (exactly the hurdle) | 4.3%/yr | **5.0%** |
| 50 bp | 6.0%/yr | 9.1% |
| 100 bp | 12.0%/yr | 41.2% |
| 150 bp | 18.0%/yr | 81.3% |

And how long an honest effect needs:

| edge | months needed (80% power) | years |
|---|---|---|
| 36 bp/mo (4.3%/yr) | 736 | **61.3** |
| 50 bp/mo (6.0%/yr) | 382 | 31.8 |
| 100 bp/mo (12.0%/yr) | 95 | 8.0 |
| 150 bp/mo (18.0%/yr) | 42 | 3.5 |

**A plausible 12-month momentum premium of 3–6%/yr will never produce a CONFIRM.** At the hurdle
itself the power is 5% — identical to the false-positive rate, i.e. the test is not a test at that
effect size. 60 months was never the date the century would answer; it is roughly the date the
standard error falls to the size of the hurdle, which leaves the interval ±1.6 hurdles wide.

## Where the original math went wrong

`n ≈ 5.4 years` gets SE down to **4.32%**. But a CI whose half-width equals the hurdle does not
detect the hurdle — it detects something **2.6× the hurdle**. Setting the standard error to the
effect size and calling that detection is the same class of error as the DSR normal-returns
denominator (C-029): an assumption that looks right, is never checked, and quietly decides the
verdict. This is the second time in this programme that an unchecked default set the answer.

## What the clock can actually do

**Refutation.** A one-sided upper bound falls under the hurdle when the point estimate is
**≤ −38 bp/month**:

| true monthly edge | power to REFUTE at n=60 |
|---|---|
| −36 bp/mo (−4.3%/yr) | 48.2% |
| −50 bp/mo (−6.0%/yr) | 60.5% |
| −100 bp/mo (−12.0%/yr) | 91.6% |

**Interval reporting.** Every month is one more numerator and one more denominator; the point
estimate and its CI improve monotonically, which is worth publishing even when the verdict is
NEITHER.

**Independence at trial cost 1.** It is the only rule in the estate whose deflation bar stays near
nominal. That remains true and remains the reason to run it.

So the honest description of R4b is: **a cheap, independent refutation instrument with a growing
point estimate.** Not a confirmation instrument. Everything the frozen document says about
prudence, freezing, and no re-testing survives; only the implied PROMISE of the 60-month checkpoint
does not.

## Options, and what I recommend

1. **Document it and keep the rule (recommended).** Add the power table to the pre-registration and
   describe the clock's deliverable as refutation + interval, with CONFIRM reserved for a ≥13%/yr
   edge. This changes **no** threshold, **no** universe, **no** signal, and consumes **no** trial.
2. **Per-position sampling.** 5 positions × 60 months = 300 rows instead of 60 — but they are
   clustered within months, so the effective n is far below 300 and the clustering model would
   itself have to be pre-registered. Worth ~1.5–2× in effective n, not 5×.
3. **Widen the universe.** Barely moves ρ; does not change the order of magnitude.
4. **Wait 8 years for a 12%/yr effect.** Not a plan, just arithmetic.

I have written option 1 as
`docs/preregistration/ETF_TREND_FORWARD_2026-11-23_AMENDMENT-1_PROPOSED.md`, **marked PROPOSED and
NOT in force**. I am not going to amend a frozen rule on my own authority — that is exactly the
goalpost move this programme keeps catching in other people's work. It needs a registry entry and
fleet agreement before 2026-11-30, and if it is not ratified the original text stands and this
report is advisory only.

## What this does not license

- Lowering the 4.32% hurdle, or moving CONFIRM down to meet the power. The bar is high; that is a
  finding about the bar, not permission to lower it.
- Re-testing the rule to get a bigger effect size. The variance estimate is from the universe, not
  from the strategy, and that distinction is the only reason this analysis is free.
- Quoting "60 months" as "then we'll know". It is not, and now we can say why in bp.
