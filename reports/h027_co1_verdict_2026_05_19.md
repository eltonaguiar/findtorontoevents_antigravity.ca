## Why adding DBA/DBB would not change the verdict

DBA (agricultural ETF) and DBB (base metals ETF) have 168 records each with WR 48.4% and 51.8% respectively — both based on synthetic seasonal-proxy data (USDA FAS PSD empty, LME not wired). Even pooling them in:

- DBA wins 341/704 = 48.4%
- DBB wins 365/704 = 51.8%
- 6-proxy pooled WR: (7816+341+365)/(16001+704+704) = 8522/17409 = 48.9%

6/6 WR is still 48.9% < 50% floor. The agricultural/metals proxies cannot rescue the signal.

## Root cause analysis

The CO-1 signal (inventory surprise z-score vs trailing-seasonal proxy) has no detectable edge across any of the 6 ETF proxies or any of the 14-day walk-forward windows tested. This is likely because:

1. **Expectation proxy is too weak**: The trailing-seasonal expectation proxy is not a real analyst-consensus feed (no free source exists). The "surprise" is therefore not a true surprise relative to the market.
2. **Fast mean-reversion**: The headline inventory number moves prices intraday on release. By the next day's close (the entry used here), the information is fully priced. The 1D drift signal is zero.
3. **ETF layer absorbs the signal**: ETF tracking error and management fees add costs that eat any marginal inventory-driven edge.
